Sailing the Aegean.

Bare rock, white villages and a hard blue sea. The Aegean asks more of a crew than the Ionian — and gives back the Greece everyone pictures.

Leaving Athens, you have a choice to make within the first two hours. Turn south-west into the Saronic Gulf and you get a gentle, harbour-rich week among pine-covered islands an hour apart. Turn south-east and you are pointed at the Cyclades — bare, bright, windy and unforgettable.

Both start from our Athens base. Both are sailed in deeper blue water than the Ionian, with longer passages, stronger wind and villages that look like the photographs. The Aegean is where you go when the sailing itself is part of the point.

Conditions

The meltemi decides.

WindF4–7 N

The meltemi blows from the north through high summer, often for days. Shoulder months are lighter and more variable.

Day's run25–40 nm

Longer legs between island groups. Plan for real sailing days, and start early when it is blowing.

Saronic10–20 nm

The sheltered alternative — short hops between Aegina, Poros, Hydra and Spetses.

SeasonMay–Oct

May, June, September and October are the kindest. July and August are the windiest and the busiest.

The islands

Two very different weeks.

01

Aegina

An hour and a half from base, and the easiest first night of any Aegean charter. Pistachio groves, a working town harbour, and the fishing village of Perdika on the south-west corner for a quieter evening.

02

Poros

Separated from the mainland by a channel a few hundred metres wide, with a clock tower above tiers of terracotta roofs. Sheltered whatever the wind does, and a favourite for a relaxed second night.

03

Hydra

No cars, no scooters — donkeys and stone. A perfect stone amphitheatre of a harbour that fills early in summer, so arrive at lunchtime or anchor outside and take the tender in.

04

Spetses & Ermioni

The southern end of the Saronic. Spetses has grand old captains’ houses and horse-drawn carriages; Ermioni, opposite, is a quiet mainland peninsula with tavernas on both shores.

05

Kea & Kythnos

The gateway to the Cyclades. Vourkari on Kea is a smart, sheltered inlet; Kythnos hides one of the best anchorages in Greece at Kolona, a sandbar with sea on both sides.

06

Serifos & Sifnos

Serifos rises to a white village stacked on a cone of rock above the port. Sifnos, its neighbour, is the food island of the Cyclades — and Vathi bay on its south coast is a fine, protected night stop.

07

Milos

Volcanic and strange, with cliffs the colour of bone at Kleftiko and coloured fishing garages cut into the rock. Further south, and worth the extra day it costs you.

08

Paros, Naxos & Mykonos

The heart of the Cyclades: Naoussa’s little fishing port, Naxos’ marble gate above the harbour, the ancient site of Delos, and Mykonos when you want one loud night in the middle of a quiet week.

Sample itinerary

Seven days in the Saronic.

The route we recommend to crews sailing the Aegean for the first time, or sailing in July and August.

  1. Day 1

    Athens → Aegina

    A short first leg out of the Saronic. Perdika for a quiet quay and grilled fish, or Aegina town if you want life around you.

  2. Day 2

    Aegina → Poros

    Down the coast to the narrow channel at Poros. Anchor in Russian Bay for a swim on the way in.

  3. Day 3

    Poros → Hydra

    A proper sail across to the most photogenic harbour in Greece. Arrive early — the quay is small and the town does not grow.

  4. Day 4

    Hydra → Spetses

    Further south-west, with a lunch anchorage in Dokos or the bays of the Argolic shore on the way.

  5. Day 5

    Spetses → Ermioni

    A short hop to the mainland peninsula, with tavernas on both sides of the isthmus and a sunset walk through the pines.

  6. Day 6

    Ermioni → Poros or Epidaurus

    North again, with a possible detour ashore to the ancient theatre at Epidaurus if the timing works.

  7. Day 7

    Back to Athens

    An early start with the land breeze and a straightforward run back to base for the morning handover.

Ten days or more? Swap the Saronic for the western Cyclades — Kea, Kythnos, Serifos, Sifnos and back — or push on to Paros and Naxos and let the meltemi push you home.

Good to know

Aegean questions.

What is the meltemi and when does it blow?

The meltemi is a dry northerly wind that dominates the Aegean from roughly mid-June to mid-September. It can blow force 5–7 for several days at a time, sometimes more between the islands. It is a fine sailing wind for an experienced crew, and the reason we suggest the Saronic Gulf to everyone else in high summer.

Can you sail the Cyclades in one week?

You can reach the western Cyclades — Kea, Kythnos, Serifos and Sifnos — comfortably in a week from Athens. A full circuit taking in Paros, Naxos and Mykonos is better suited to ten days or more, because the meltemi can pin you in a harbour for a day.

Which route suits a first Aegean charter?

The Saronic Gulf. Aegina, Poros, Hydra, Ermioni and Spetses are close together, sheltered from the worst of the meltemi, and full of harbours — a week there is relaxed even in August.

Is a catamaran a good idea in the Aegean?

For comfort at anchor, yes — and the shallow draft opens up bays a monohull cannot use. In a strong meltemi a catamaran is more affected by windage when berthing, so we would usually put a skipper aboard for a first Aegean week.
